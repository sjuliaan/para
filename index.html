Module 11: Leishmania & Trypanosoma (Hemoflagellates)

Transmitted by blood-sucking insect vectors. Includes 4 main morphological stages: Amastigote, Promastigote, Epimastigote, Trypomastigote.

Hemoflagellate Morphological Stages Quick Matrix Intracellular vs Extracellular
Stage Location in Host Key Features Parasite Presence
Amastigote Intracellular (Macrophages, RES, Muscle, CNS) Small (3-5 µm), ovoid, non-motile, large nucleus, kinetoplast with axoneme. Leishmania spp., Trypanosoma cruzi
Promastigote Arthropod vector (Sandfly) / NNN Culture Elongated, extracellular, single anterior free flagellum (15-28 µm). Motile. Leishmania spp. (Infective stage)
Epimastigote Arthropod vector midgut/salivary glands Kinetoplast anterior to nucleus, short undulating membrane. T. cruzi, T. brucei complex
Trypomastigote Peripheral Blood Circulation (Extracellular) C/U/S shape, posterior kinetoplast, full undulating membrane with free flagellum. T. cruzi, T. brucei complex

Leishmania Species

Sandfly Vector
  • Old World Vector: Phlebotomus sandfly (Asia, Africa, Europe).
  • New World Vector: Lutzomyia sandfly (Central/South America).
  • Infective Stage: Promastigote (in Sandfly proboscis).
  • Diagnostic Stage: Amastigote (intracellular LD bodies in macrophages).

Clinical Forms & High-Yield Key Points:

• Cutaneous (CL): "Oriental sore" / Chiclero ulcer. Papule → violaceous ulcer w/ raised borders.

• Mucocutaneous (MCL): L. braziliensis. Espundia (destruction of nasal septum/palate).

• Visceral (VL / Kala-azar): L. donovani complex. Double quotidian fever, hepatosplenomegaly, skin darkening.

• Post-Kala-Azar Dermal Leishmaniasis (PKDL): Hypopigmented macules/nodules years post-VL.

• Montenegro Skin Test: (+) ≥5mm in CL & MCL; (-) in DCL & active Visceral Leishmaniasis.

Trypanosoma cruzi

Chagas Disease
  • Vector: Triatomine bug / Reduviid bug / "Kissing bug" (Triatoma, Rhodnius, Panstrongylus).
  • Transmission Mode: Stercorarian transmission (feces rubbed into bite wound/conjunctiva).
  • Infective Stage: Metacyclic Trypomastigote (in bug feces).
  • Diagnostic Stage: Trypomastigote in blood (C/U/S shaped w/ large kinetoplast) & Amastigote in tissue.

Must-Know Clinical Signs & Diagnosis:

• Romaña's Sign: Unilateral painless palpebral edema (eyelid swelling) + conjunctivitis.

• Chagoma: Erythematous subcutaneous nodule at bite site.

• Chronic Phase: Apical Chagasic Cardiomyopathy, Megacolon, Megaesophagus.

• Xenodiagnosis: Uninfected triatomine bugs allowed to feed on patient, then intestinal content examined after 30 days.

• DOC: Nifurtimox and Benznidazole.

Module 12: Plasmodium Species (Malaria Parasites)

Vector: Female Anopheles minimus var. flavirostris (night biter in slow-flowing streams). Man = Intermediate Host; Mosquito = Definitive (Final) Host.

Complications & Pathophysiology

• Cytoadherence & Rosetting: PfEMP-1 knobs on P. falciparum RBCs adhere to ICAM-1 on microvascular endothelium causing microvascular obstruction & tissue anoxia.

• Blackwater Fever: Massive intravascular hemolysis, hemoglobinuria (dark brown/black urine), acute renal failure.

• Cerebral Malaria: Sequestration in brain capillaries -> coma, convulsions (15-20% mortality).

Laboratory Diagnosis

• Thick Blood Smear: GOLD STANDARD for SCREENING (dehemoglobinized w/ water to lyse RBCs).

• Thin Blood Smear: GOLD STANDARD for SPECIES IDENTIFICATION (fixed w/ methanol).

• QBC (Quantitative Buffy Coat): Acridine orange fluorescent dye binds parasite nucleic acid in capillary tube.

• RDT Antigens: PfHRP2 (P. falciparum specific), pLDH (Plasmodium lactate dehydrogenase), PMA (Pan-malarial antigen).

Protective Genetic Factors

• Sickle Cell Trait (HbS) / HbC / HbE: Protects against severe P. falciparum malaria.

• Duffy Blood Group Negative [Fy(a-b-)]: Complete resistance to P. vivax infection (lacks receptor for invasion).

• G6PD Deficiency: Increased oxidative stress limits intracellular schizogony.

• Hereditary Ovalocytosis & Thalassemia: Reduces cytoadherence & invasion.
